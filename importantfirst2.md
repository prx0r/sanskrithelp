That means:

- **not** a huge theatrical chant
- **not** over-long held notes
- **not** forced breath

### Why?
Because right now your aim is:

**phoneme → articulation → body locus → memory**

not full ritual performance.

## 4) How long to hold each sound?

Use a **short, clear, natural utterance**.

### Good rule:
- short vowels: about **1 beat**
- long vowels: about **2 beats**
- consonants: speak as ordinary Sanskrit syllables, e.g. **ka**, **kha**, **ga**
- anusvāra / visarga: let them sound clearly but briefly

So:

- **a** = short
- **ā** = slightly longer
- **ka** = clear, concise
- **kṣa** = deliberate but not dragged out

Abhinavagupta-side adjacent material points more toward **sound carried on comfortable breath, repeated with awareness**, not body-tormenting prāṇāyāma or artificially prolonged chanting.

So I’d use:

> **one natural inhale, one clear sound on the exhale, then a brief silence.**

---

## 5) The exact practice format I recommend

For each phoneme:

1. See the glyph.
2. Know the locus.
3. Take a natural inhale.
4. On the exhale, speak the sound once.
5. Feel the articulation.
6. Touch or visualize the body point.
7. Pause 1–2 seconds in silence.
8. Move on.

That’s the whole machine.
