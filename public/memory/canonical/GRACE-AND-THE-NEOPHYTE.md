## 1. Grace does the work — you don't earn it, you receive it

The foundation of the whole system, stated before anything else is taught:

> "The Lord's grace *(bhagavatprasāda)* alone is the instrumental means *(nimitta)*
> by which what has been seen by others can be elucidated."
> — ahnika-01 (`tantraloka-complete.txt` ~8433)

> "The (kāraṇa) of liberation is Śiva's grace. Everything else that may serve as a
> means to it is [secondary]."
> — ahnika-01 (~11031)

Grace is not a mood. It is Śiva's own nature turned toward you — *anugrahaśakti*,
the power-of-grace, one of His five acts (creation, persistence, obscuration,
revelation, grace). "Supreme Lord's nature is grace" (ahnika-01 ~6380).

**The command is the empowerment.** When a teacher (or deity, or text) gives you
the order to practice, the order itself carries the power:

> "To receive such a command is like receiving a descent of the power of grace
> *(śaktipāta)*… one is empowered, and so is given the authority *(adhikāra)*
> to do what has been commanded."
> — ahnika-01 (~8425)

Read that twice. *Adhikāra* — qualification — is not a certificate you study for.
It is conferred by the encounter itself. If these practices have found you and
you feel the pull, that pull *is* the beginning of the qualification.

And the aim of the most intense grace is not heaven later but freedom now:

> "The aim… by the grace of the descent of the Lord's supreme power, is solely
> liberation in this life *(jīvanmukti)*… penetrating absorption *(samāveśa)*
> in the unity which is the inherent nature of Bhairava."
> — ahnika-01 (~8825, on TĀ 1 + Spandakārikā 30)

## 2. Three intensities — find yours honestly

TĀ 13.129–132 (Sanskrit via GRETIL):

> *tāratamyaprakāśo yastīvramadhyamamandatāḥ* — grace comes in degrees:
> intense, middling, slow. (13.129)
>
> *tīvratīvraḥ śaktipāto dehapātavaśātsvayam* — by the most intense descent,
> freedom comes of itself, at the fall of the body (at latest). (13.130)
>
> *madhyatīvrātpunaḥ sarvamajñānaṃ vinivartate* — by the middling-intense,
> all ignorance ceases. (13.131)
>
> *tatprātibhaṃ mahājñānaṃ śāstrācāryānapekṣi yat* — this is the great knowledge
> of intuitive insight *(pratibhā)*, independent of scriptures and teachers. (13.132)

What each means for you:

- **Intense *(tīvra)*:** practice becomes absorption almost by itself. Rare. If
  sitting still already drops you into recognition, stop reading guides.
- **Middling *(madhya)*:** ignorance dissolves through study + reflection, and at
  some point insight starts arriving *unasked* — you understand a verse before
  the commentary explains it. "Sound Reasoning arises by itself" (ahnika-04 ~3928).
  This is the realistic target of sustained wheel + text work.
- **Slow *(manda)*:** the gradual path — and here is the passage that should
  encourage every solitary beginner:

> "One [descent] is direct, that leads directly to a true Trika teacher. Another
> is gradual, that is, takes place in stages, as the seeker is led to more than
> one teacher. Initially, the first teacher may not even be a Śaivite. But then,
> by Śiva's grace, he is led to a true Śaiva teacher… from whom he receives
> initiation into Śaiva schools of progressively higher levels."
> — ahnika-04 (~3298, on TĀ 4.37 + 13/302)

Your "teachers" right now are a wheel, a grammar, and a dead Kashmiri master
whose book survived a thousand years to reach your disk. The text explicitly
allows the path to work through such stages. Lower schools are not wasted time;
grace uses them as stairs.

## 3. The neophyte — initiation, worthiness, and the body that trembles

**Two initiations.** The first, *samayadīkṣā*, "introduces the neophyte into the
religion and obliges them to follow its rules *(samaya)* but does not confer
liberation, for which another initiation, *nirvāṇadīkṣā*, is required"
(ahnika-15 ~1275, citing Goodall on the Śiva-hand rite).
Translation for us: *learning the rules and keeping them* (daily practice, vows
of regularity) is the first initiation, and it is real — but don't mistake
collecting practices for freedom.

**It works even in the unworthy.** "Initiation has positive results, even in
those who receive it are unworthy" (ahnika-15 ~191). Start before you feel ready.
But note the one blockage named just above it: "one who is consumed by greed
and the like does not receive a descent of the power of grace." Greed — grasping
at powers, experiences, status — closes the channel. Curiosity and love open it.

**The teacher checks the body.** At initiation the guru watches for outer signs
"beginning with the trembling of the body, to make sure that it [penetration]
has taken place" (complete ~25585; cf. TĀ 5/101ff). The tradition always
demanded *felt evidence*, not claimed understanding. For solitary work the
principle survives: a practice is working when something involuntary happens —
breath changes, tremor, tears, sudden stillness. No sensation, no penetration;
that is information, not failure. Adjust and continue.

**The work exists because students asked.** Abhinavagupta says he composed the
Tantrāloka because "requested by (my) sincere disciples and fellows" (ahnika-01
~8900, on the *sambandha* verses). The colossal text is downstream of
beginners' honest questions. Asking is part of the method.

## 4. The teacher — how to recognize one, including in yourself

**True and false teachers are your grace made visible:**

> "The distinction between a true or false teacher or scripture effectively
> amounts to the variety of the descent of the power of grace [you] have
> received… the teachers that belong to other systems are governed by the
> deluding power of Māyā called Vāmā, whereas our teachers are sustained by
> the illumining power Jyesthā."
> — ahnika-04 (~3318, on TĀ 4.37)

A teacher is known by what governs them and by their fruits — do their students
become freer, clearer, kinder? Apply this to books, videos, and your own inner
commentary with equal strictness.

**The ladder of authority (Kiraṇatantra, quoted approvingly):**

> "Convincing insight comes from the teacher, the scripture and oneself; of
> these, that which comes later in this series is more important *(mukhya)*,
> while that which comes earlier serves as a means to that which follows…
> Thus one's own insight is the ultimate means… the only one."
> — ahnika-04 (~3890)

Teacher < scripture < your own insight. And lest that sound like permission to
discard guidance: "By this is not meant that one should not have a teacher or
learn from scriptures. Rather they are effective guides **by virtue of one's
own insight that develops by Śiva's grace**, which as His divine power is the
Goddess."

This is the charter for your whole project: wheel (teacher-function) <
Abhinavagupta (scripture-function) < the recognition that dawns in you. The
first two exist to provoke the third.

**There are teachers who need no support** *(nirbhitti)* — established directly
in insight — and those who lean on method *(sahabhittika)* (TĀ 4.47–48). Prefer
the first kind; forgive yourself for being the second kind for a long time.

## 5. Devotion and repetition — the actual engine

Abhinavagupta's instructional refrain, TĀ 4.7:

> *bhūyo bhūyo vimṛśatāṃ jāyate tatsphuṭātmatā* — "Bhairava's radiant power,
> which is consciousness, becomes fully clear to those who **reflect upon it
> again and again**." (GRETIL AbhT_4.7; ahnika-04 ~642)

Not once. Not intensely once. Again and again — that is the whole of *vimalīkaraṇa*
in one line, and it is also exactly how a wheel becomes a reflex.

**Purified thought illuminates instead of obscuring:**

> "Thought thus purified not only ceases to obscure consciousness; it also
> contributes to its illumination. The yogi moves freely from savikalpa to
> nirvikalpa consciousness and back, radiant with the grace of Bhairava, who is
> his true nature." — ahnika-04 (~620, on TĀ 4.2–7)

This is why the Pāṇini/Bruno track is legitimate sādhana and not mere study:
*saṃskāra of vikalpa* — training one thought-construction to produce a purer
one — is Śāktopāya itself (TĀ 4.2). A derivation watched until it becomes
transparent is a vikalpa watching itself dissolve. Same muscle.

**The mirror (TĀ 4.207–208, Sanskrit via GRETIL):**

> *yathā puraḥsthe mukure nijaṃ vaktraṃ vibhāvayan / bhūyo bhūyastadekātma
> vaktraṃ vetti nijātmanaḥ* — "As one attentively beholds one's own face
> in a mirror set before one, and again and again recognizes that face as
> none other than oneself… (207)
>
> *tathā vikalpamukure dhyānapūjārcanātmani / ātmānaṃ bhairavaṃ
> paśyannacirāttanmayībhavet* — so, in the mirror of vikalpa — meditation,
> worship, contemplation — seeing oneself as Bhairava, one swiftly becomes
> of that very nature." (208)

The imagined Sanskrit world is such a mirror: constructed deliberately, gazed
into repeatedly, until recognition is immediate — "just as one recognizes
oneself in the reflection of one's face in a mirror" (complete ~59440).
Then the mirror is optional. Mastery is the palace appearing instantly when
useful and vanishing instantly when not.

**The three means, one sentence each** (TĀ 1.168–170):
*Śāmbhava* — penetration arising for one who thinks of nothing, by intense
awakening. *Śākta* — penetration attained thinking reality with mind alone, no
mantra-utterance needed. *Āṇava* — penetration by uccāra, karaṇa, dhyāna,
varṇa-sthāna-kalpanā (utterance, yogic instruments, visualization, cycling the
phonemes through their loci). Your wheel work is āṇava with the serial numbers
filed in; the sequence points beyond itself.

## 6. Obstacles — what will actually go wrong

Abhinavagupta is blunt: obstacles strike *practitioners*, not just dilettantes.

> "What mindful person sanctified by the Supreme Lord's descent of power would
> not engage in practice? … But obstacles arise also for one who is applying
> himself… the Vināyakas conjoin him to transitory worldly pleasures."
> — ahnika-01 (~8871, on the opening maṅgala verses)

Expect the classic interference pattern right after practice deepens:
restlessness, sudden busyness, sexual/aggressive static, intellectual contempt
for the practice ("this is just flashcards"). He prescribes Gaṇeśa at the
threshold for a reason — begin sessions with an explicit clearing gesture,
whatever your idiom.

Two sociological notes, both useful:

- **"People without sensible views and with tastes other than ours are not our
  own people"** (complete ~28005, TĀ 1.193 — *nāsmākaṃ… bhinnaruciḥ*). Don't
  argue about the practice with people who don't share the appetite. Protect
  the work by keeping it quiet.
- **Sanctified by grace, "free of obstacles by virtue of the greatness of Pure
  Knowledge, he ultimately ascends onto the true path"** — even one who started
  in an inferior system (ahnika-04 ~3450). A bad start doesn't bind; only
  stopping does.

## 7. For you, now — a working summary

1. **Grace first, effort second.** Sit down each session as a response to the
   pull, not as self-improvement. Name it silently if it helps: this hour is
   *prasāda*, not productivity.
2. **Keep the samaya minimal and absolute.** Two phonemes a night kept beats
   fifty letters crammed. The neophyte's vow is regularity, not intensity.
3. **Demand felt evidence.** After each install, check the body: breath, tremor,
   warmth, stillness. Log it (your practice-log already has the types). No sign
   for a week = change the method, not yourself.
4. **Climb the Kiraṇa ladder deliberately.** Wheel until automatic → text until
   understood → sit until insight arrives unasked *(pratibhā)*. When your own
   reflection contradicts the wheel, trust the reflection and check the wheel —
   that is the system working, not breaking.
5. **Reflect again and again** *(bhūyo bhūyaḥ)*. The wheel is scaffolding:
   see it → rotate it mentally → feel relations without rotating → hear without
   imagery → watch the impulse from which sound/thought arises → hold the whole
   matrix without losing the ground. Geometry optional at the end, available
   on demand.
6. **Watch for the Vināyakas.** Interference after depth is confirmation, not
   refutation. Clear the threshold, continue.
7. **Greed blocks; love conducts.** Wanting powers, speed, or status closes the
   channel. Wanting to *know the letters as they are* opens it. Audit motive
   monthly.

> *yathā vikalpamukure… ātmānaṃ bhairavaṃ paśyannacirāttanmayībhavet* —
> in the mirror of constructed thought, seeing oneself as Bhairava, one swiftly
> becomes of that very nature. (TĀ 4.208)

The mirror you are building out of wheels and derivations is that mirror.
Build it well, gaze often, and let it dissolve when recognition comes.
